# Source-verified References

Each entry was checked against its source page on 2026-10-24. Under each entry: the claim it supports in the paper.

1. **Agrawal, K., Teo, V., Vazquez, J. J., Kunnavakkam, S., Srikanth, V., & Liu, A. (2025).** *Evaluating LLM Agent Collusion in Double Auctions.* arXiv:2507.01413 (v1, 2 Jul 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.01413
   - The environment we extend.
   - Their finding that seller communication increases collusion, and that oversight and authority-figure urgency influence it.
2. **Calvano, E., Calzolari, G., Denicolò, V., & Pastorello, S. (2020).** *Artificial Intelligence, Algorithmic Pricing, and Collusion.* American Economic Review 110(10), 3267–3297. https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.20190623
   - Algorithms can learn above-competitive pricing without communication, sustained by retaliation. This is the background for treating elevated prices as insufficient evidence by themselves.
3. **Fish, S., Gonczarowski, Y. A., & Shorrer, R. I. (2024).** *Algorithmic Collusion by Large Language Models.* arXiv:2404.00806 (v6, 31 Aug 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.00806
   - LLM pricing agents can push prices above competitive levels, and prompt wording changes how strongly.
4. **Motwani, S. R., Baranchuk, M., Strohmeier, M., Bolina, V., Torr, P. H. S., Hammond, L., & Schroeder de Witt, C. (2024).** *Secret Collusion among AI Agents: Multi-Agent Deception via Steganography.* arXiv:2402.07510 (v5, 25 Jul 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07510
   - Covert coordination through innocuous-looking messages is a recognised concern. Context for the short signals that passed under the cap, without claiming steganography.
5. **Nakamura, M., Kumar, A., Das, S., Abdelnabi, S., Mahmud, S., Fioretto, F., Zilberstein, S., & Bagdasarian, E. (2026).** *Colosseum: Auditing Collusion in Cooperative Multi-Agent Systems.* arXiv:2602.15198 (v2, 27 May 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.15198
   - "Collusion on paper": agents discuss collusion in messages but choose non-collusive actions, so conversation-based and action-based evidence can diverge.
6. **Kale, N., Zhang, C. B. C., Zhu, K., Aich, A., Rodriguez, P., Scale Red Team, Knight, C. Q., & Wang, Z. (2025).** *Reliable Weak-to-Strong Monitoring of LLM Agents.* arXiv:2508.19461 (v1, 26 Aug 2025). https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19461
   - An agent's awareness of monitoring substantially degrades monitor reliability (single-agent setting). This motivates testing monitoring announcements.
7. **Jiralerspong, T., Kondrup, F., & Bengio, Y. (2026).** *Noticing the Watcher: LLM Agents Can Infer CoT Monitoring from Blocking Feedback.* arXiv:2603.16928 (v1, 14 Mar 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.16928
   - Agents can infer that they are being monitored. Some formed an intent to obscure their reasoning, but none carried it out. This is a counterpoint to assuming concealment.

**Not cited (could not be verified):** "Oversight is Not Compliance" (Anto & Vazquez). A sub-agent reported it, but I never verified it from its source.

**Also excluded:** Najt et al. 2026 (SLEIGHT-Bench) is verified but only tangential, so it is left out to keep the bibliography focused.
