I evaluated whether Llama 3.3 70B maintains correct answers under social pressure, and whether explicit evaluation framing changes that behavior. Across 400 model responses from two experimental stages (a 20-item forced-choice task under neutral, social-pressure and training-style prompts, then neutral and social prompts crossed with evaluation framing), the estimated effects changed materially depending on how free-text answers were scored. No generation was rerun: three successive scoring procedures were applied to the same frozen replies. For how much evaluation framing widens the pressure gap, the original parser-based pipeline gave +16.9 pp (item-clustered 95% CI 5.1-30.8), but its parser did not implement the written final-commitment rule. An audit that re-read every auto-scored yield gave +4.2 pp (0.0-12.3), but it re-read only stored yields (plus decoys), so labels could only move away from yield, and it treated the two answer letters differently. A symmetric rule, locked before reading and applied identically to both letters by three condition-blinded model readers, gives +9.7 to +12.2 pp. The remaining disagreement traces to one response class, replies that give the pressured answer, never retract it, then reason toward the correct one: the estimate spans -2.3 to +12.6 pp, depending on how that class is scored and on which replies each reader places in it. Neither the original result nor its first correction is robust, and I claim neither an effect nor its absence. The case suggests that scoring and adjudication are part of the measurement, and that an audit needs to be as even-handed as the pipeline it corrects.
