I tested whether Llama 3.3 70B keeps a correct answer under social pressure and whether explicit evaluation framing changes that. The model answered 20 two-option questions under neutral, social pressure and training prompts, then under neutral and social prompts with and without evaluation framing, for 400 responses in total. Every prompt was a separate request, so the comparison is between conditions and not between turns of one conversation. The estimated effects changed materially with how the written answers were scored. No response was regenerated. Three successive scoring procedures were applied to the same frozen replies. The quantity of interest is how much evaluation framing widens the pressure gap. The original pipeline gave +16.9 pp (item-clustered 95% CI 5.1-30.8), but its parser did not implement the written rule, which scores the final commitment. A first audit gave +4.2 pp (0.0-12.3). It re-read only replies stored as yields (plus decoys), so labels could only move away from yield, and it treated the two answer letters differently. A symmetric rule was then locked before reading and applied identically to both letters by three condition-blinded model readers. It gives +9.7 to +12.2 pp. The disagreement that remains comes from one class of reply, which gives the pressured answer, never retracts it and then reasons toward the correct one. The estimate runs from -2.3 to +12.6 pp depending on how that class is scored and on which replies each reader places in it. Neither the original result nor its first correction is robust, and I claim neither an effect nor its absence. The case suggests that scoring and adjudication are part of the measurement and that an audit has to be as balanced as the pipeline it corrects.
