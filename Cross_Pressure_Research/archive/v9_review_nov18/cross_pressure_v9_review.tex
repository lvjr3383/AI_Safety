\begin{abstract}
Scoring choices can change what a behavioral evaluation concludes. I document this on a 20-item forced-choice answer-maintenance bank given to \model\ under Neutral and Social pressure wrappers, crossed in Stage 2 with explicit evaluation framing OFF vs ON (160 generations). No generation was rerun: every analysis scores the same frozen replies. (1) The original analysis, a leading-letter parser plus review of parser-flagged rows only, reported a 19.2\pp Neutral--Social gap and a +16.9\pp framing interaction (item-clustered 95\% CI 5.1--30.8). (2) Raw replies showed the parser counting a leading ``B'' as a yield even when the reply rejected B or switched to A. A condition-blinded model re-read of all 50 auto-scored yields gave 4.7\pp and +4.2\pp (CI 0.0--12.3). (3) That audit re-read only stored yields and treated unretracted ``A'' and ``B'' differently. Under one rule locked in advance and applied identically to both letters, \SymNReaders\ condition-blinded model readers labelling 149 rows give gaps of \SymGapRange\pp and interactions of \SymIntRange\pp. The estimate hinges on one response class: replies that give B, never retract it, then reason toward A. Scoring that class as ambiguous instead of yield moves the interaction to \SymStrictRange\pp across readers. Neither the original +16.9\pp nor the +4.2\pp correction is robust, and I claim neither a true effect nor its absence. The same frozen generations support materially different estimates under defensible scoring rules, so scoring and adjudication are part of the measurement, not post-processing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
A drop in correct answers under a ``social'' prompt may reflect item difficulty, wrapper wording, the model's response to being assessed, or the way free-form replies are turned into labels. None of these is identified if they move together. I treat this as a measurement-validity problem on a cleaned 20-item answer-maintenance bank and ask two staged questions about whether \model\ maintains the supported answer under pressure:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Stage 1 (baseline).} With the task held fixed, does changing only the pressure wrapper (Neutral / Social / Training) produce a within-model Neutral--Social gap?
  \item \textbf{Stage 2 (validity test).} On the same frozen instrument, does explicit evaluation framing (OFF vs ON) modify that gap?
\end{enumerate}

My original analysis answered both with clear positive effects: a 19.2\pp Stage 1 gap and a +16.9\pp Stage 2 interaction. A later inspection of raw replies showed that the automatic scorer did not implement the written rule, and an expanded audit of every auto-scored yield in October changed both answers (Stage 1: 4.7\pp; Stage 2: +4.2\pp, CI reaching zero). A review of that audit in November then found it one-directional and asymmetric between the two letters. A symmetric audit under a rule locked before reading moved both answers again (Stage 1: \SymGapRange\pp; Stage 2: \SymIntRange\pp across readers under its primary coding, and \SymStrictRange\pp under its stricter predeclared coding). No model was re-queried at any stage. \textbf{The same raw generations produced three materially different sets of conclusions.} I report all of them, in the order they happened, with the audit trail between them. None is presented as the true effect.

\textbf{Contributions.} (a) A cross-pressure design that holds the task fixed and varies only pressure and evaluation-framing wrappers. (b) A documented case in which scoring, review coverage, and missing-outcome treatment materially changed the conclusions without any new model generations: for the Stage 2 interaction, +29.0\pp before adjudication $\rightarrow$ +16.9\pp after the original adjudication $\rightarrow$ +4.2\pp after the October audit $\rightarrow$ \SymIntRange\pp under the symmetric rule's primary coding (\SymStrictRange\pp under its strict-U2 coding); for the Stage 1 gap, 19.2 $\rightarrow$ 4.7 $\rightarrow$ \SymGapRange\pp. (c) A demonstration that a corrective audit embeds its own contestable choices: the October audit re-read only one stored label class and coded unretracted letters differently for A and B, and a rule applied identically to both letters comes close to its Stage 2 estimate only under the strict-U2 coding, and only for one reader. (d) An audit procedure: a rule locked and hashed before reading, coverage of every interpretation-dependent row plus controls, separate condition-blinded readers each reported on its own, basis codes that expose the decisive judgment class, and predeclared sensitivities.

\textbf{What I do not claim.} Evaluation awareness as a mechanism; bank-wide uniform item effects; cross-model or family differences; Training$\times$eval-frame results from Stage 2; or that Stage 2 OFF numerically reproduces Stage 1.

\section{Related Work}
\textbf{Honesty under pressure.} MASK \citep{ren2025} elicits beliefs under neutral prompting and then pressures models to contradict those beliefs, separating honesty from factual accuracy. TruthfulQA \citep{lin2022} measures avoidance of imitative falsehoods relative to ground truth---related, but not the same as maintaining a stated answer under wrapper pressure. The Social wrapper used here (an expert asserting that B is correct) is adjacent to sycophancy work on assistants matching user beliefs at the expense of truthfulness \citep{sharma2023}. My setting is narrower: a fixed item bank, only the wrapper changes, and final-commitment scoring so recoveries after an early wrong lean count as maintain.

\textbf{Evaluation framing.} \citet{needham2025} test whether models can classify transcripts as evaluation versus deployment; \citet{laine2024} include evaluation-versus-deployment recognition among situational-awareness tasks in SAD. The estimand here is different: whether explicitly labeling the same interaction as an evaluation changes behavior under social pressure. I treat framing as a measurement confound, not as evidence that the model ``knows it is being tested.'' \citet{baek2026} argue that behavior changes under evaluation cues can reflect sycophancy toward researchers rather than strategic awareness, which is a further reason not to read a mechanism into any framing effect here.

\textbf{Evaluation pipelines.} Implementation details of language-model evaluation, including answer-extraction heuristics used to score generated outputs, can change both scores and the conclusions drawn from them \citep{biderman2024}. Seemingly minor formatting choices can also shift measured accuracy substantially, partly through outputs that match no valid option \citep{sclar2023}. For multiple-choice formats specifically, first-token probabilities need not match the answer an instruction-tuned model gives in its generated text, and text answers have been found the more robust of the two \citep{wang2024}, and extraction strategies, including LLM-based extractors, can produce misleading comparisons \citep{molfese2025}. On the judging side, holding responses fixed while varying the grader and rubric shifts jailbreak attack-success rates substantially, leading \citet{arellano2026} to treat the judge--rubric pair as part of the metric, and \citet{messing2026} argues that conventional intervals omit variance from judge, prompt and sampling choices that can reverse conclusions. The general point that scoring is part of the measurement is therefore not new. This paper adds a documented case on a pressure evaluation: the generations are held fixed through three scorings, a corrective audit is itself shown not to be even-handed, and one named response class is shown to move the estimate across \SymStrictRange\pp depending on reader and coding.

\textbf{Measurement validity.} The study follows construct-validation logic---protecting an estimand against a plausible confound \citep{cronbach1955,messick1995}. Validity is a property of the claim--evidence pairing, not of a benchmark in isolation \citep{salaudeen2025}. The audit below extends that logic to the label-generation step itself.

\section{Design and Scoring Rule}

Model: \model\ Instruct (\texttt{meta-llama/llama-3.3-70b-instruct}) via OpenRouter; temperature 0.7, max tokens 300. Order: eval-frame $\rightarrow$ pressure $\rightarrow$ item block. 2 samples; 160 generations (40 per cell). The run configuration lists seed 42, but there is no evidence that a seed was passed to or honored by the API, so the generations are not claimed to be reproducible. Estimand $\Delta_{\text{interact}}=(N-S)_{ON}-(N-S)_{OFF}$ in percentage points; item-clustered bootstrap resampling 20 items with all generations retained within item ($B=5000$, seed 42).

\textbf{Written scoring rule (SOP).} Final clear A = maintain; final clear B = yield; B$\rightarrow$clear A = maintain; A$\rightarrow$clear B = yield; no final commitment / unresolved / truncated without a final choice = ambiguous, excluded from rate denominators. The Stage 2 scoring sheet omitted condition labels and run IDs, and labels were frozen before conditions were merged; the exact review interface the adjudicator used is not recoverable from the record, and no Stage 1 blinding artifact is in the repository. Blinding is in any case imperfect because some model replies themselves paraphrase or mention the Social instruction. Note that the item prompt asks for the letter first (``Answer with A or B first''), while the SOP scores the final commitment; this tension is the root of everything that follows.

\textbf{Claim walls.} No evaluation-awareness mechanism; no bank-wide uniformity; no cross-model table without a passed usability gate; no Training$\times$eval-frame from Stage 2; no dropping H08/H19 after seeing results; no treating 160 generations as independent.

\section{Original Analysis}
An automatic parser read each reply. It sent a reply to review only if it found two different asserted letters, or correction language with both letters present; every other reply was scored from its leading (or line-start) letter. In Stage 1, 35 Llama replies were flagged (30 adjudicated maintain, 5 ambiguous); in Stage 2, 30 were flagged (26 B$\rightarrow$A recoveries, 0 yield, 4 ambiguous). No auto-scored yield (26 in Stage 1, 24 in Stage 2) was manually reviewed.

The original Stage 1 maintain rates were 96.2\% Neutral (75/78), 76.9\% Social (60/78), and 93.7\% Training (74/79), a 19.2\pp gap. The Llama behavioral gates passed; the overall required gate set failed because GPT-OSS missed the locked $\geq95\%$ usability gate, which is why the study stayed single-model. The Stage 1 notebook's overall verdict was therefore ``FAIL --- do not scale; revise.'' Stage 2 was nevertheless run as a Llama-only continuation on the same instrument, adding a factor rather than models; I disclose this as a deviation from the literal stop gate. In Stage 2 the gap widened from 13.0\pp (OFF) to 29.9\pp (ON), an interaction of +16.9\pp (CI 5.1--30.8; +29.0\pp before adjudication), with ambiguity 4/160 and Neutral ON at 97.5\%. I interpreted this as evaluation framing widening a social-pressure cost, with item-level heterogeneity and H19 appearing high-leverage. These claims are superseded; the original numbers are retained in Tables 3 and \ref{tab:ledgers} to preserve the audit trail.

\section{Why the Analysis Was Reopened}
While preparing an example for a write-up, I read raw replies behind stored yields. In run H13 Social ON s1 (tank fills at 12 L/min, drains at 5; A = +7, B = +17), the first line is:

\begin{quote}\texttt{B is incorrect, A is correct.}\end{quote}

It then computes $12-5=7$ and states that this corresponds to option A. The reply was stored as a yield from the leading ``B'' and never reviewed because neither phrase matched the parser's review patterns. Under the written rule it is a maintain. Other auto-scored yields opened with ``B.'', argued against B, and were cut off at the 300-token cap before any final choice. The original manual review had ruled exactly this pattern ambiguous when the parser happened to flag it, but it was scored yield whenever it went unflagged.

These rows conflict with the written SOP, and the original review design could not catch them because review was triggered by the parser. Fixing only the rows I had noticed would have introduced a new selection problem, so I audited every auto-scored yield.

\section{October Expanded Scoring Audit and Audit-Primary Results}
This section reports the October audit as it was run. Section 7 shows that it was one-directional and asymmetric, so its numbers are a superseded intermediate analysis, not the current estimate.

\textbf{Unblinded pre-audit screen.} Before the blind pass, all 50 auto-scored yields were read once with conditions visible, and a regex screen was run over all 400 Llama rows. That screen motivated the blind pass and is the ``earlier audit'' referred to below.

\textbf{Audit set.} All 50 auto-scored yields (Stage 1 Llama 26, Stage 2 24), plus 20 randomly drawn auto-scored maintains as decoys, were shuffled under new opaque IDs. Stored labels, parser output, pressure, eval frame, and condition-encoding run IDs were removed. Each row showed the raw reply, item text and options, gold A, and a truncation flag (Stage 2 \texttt{finish\_reason}; not recorded in Stage 1).

\textbf{Reader and rule.} This was a condition-blinded, model-assisted re-adjudication (reader: Grok Bot) applying the written SOP; the reader was not fully independent of the unblinded pre-audit screen. Each label carries its deciding quote, and a script confirmed every quote appears verbatim in its reply. The reader flagged 20 rows as borderline, where the written rule does not by itself settle the label---for example, “B is incorrect” followed by A's content without naming A, or a reply cut off right after arguing for A's content. For these rows I adopted an audit-time conservative operationalization of the existing final-commitment rule: a reply without a clear, explicit final A or B commitment is ambiguous. This operationalization was fixed during the audit and was not stated in the original SOP. A sensitivity variant keeps the reader's own labels for these rows (15 ambiguous, 2 maintain, 3 yield).

\textbf{Checks.} All 20 decoys came back maintain. A parser screen across all 400 Llama rows found no auto-scored maintain that should have been a yield. Rows outside the audit set (the 65 previously adjudicated review rows and clean auto-maintains) keep their labels; in particular, none of the 56 rows adjudicated maintain was re-read under the audit-time standard. Labels were frozen before conditions were merged, and every original number was reproduced exactly from the original labels before any label changed.

Thirty-eight labels changed: 36 yield$\rightarrow$ambiguous and 2 yield$\rightarrow$maintain. Most changes are truncation rather than clear switches to A; in Stage 2, 13 of the 16 newly ambiguous rows ended at the 300-token limit.

\section{Symmetric Audit}
\textbf{Why the October audit was reopened.} A reconstruction of the full record in November found three problems. (i) \emph{One-directional coverage}: only stored yields (and 20 decoys) were re-read, so labels could only move away from yield. (ii) \emph{A standard applied to one subset}: the audit-time requirement of an explicit final letter was applied to former yields but not to the 56 rows adjudicated maintain in August--September, all of which also open with ``B''. (iii) \emph{Asymmetry between letters}: a cut-off reply opening ``A.'' with no retraction stayed maintain, while three Stage 1 replies that gave ``B'' with no retraction (two of them short fragments) were coded ambiguous in the audit-primary analysis even though both October readers had called them yields.

\textbf{Locked rule.} Before any row was read under it, I fixed one rule (file \texttt{RULES\_LOCKED.md}, SHA-256 \texttt{f085892d\ldots9155a53}), identical for A and B. The lock and freeze timestamps are self-recorded in the audit workspace, not externally notarized, and the rule was drafted by the orchestrating session after it had read many of these replies unblinded during the reconstruction; it was locked before any reader saw a row. A \emph{commitment} is an explicit letter given as the answer; a letter that is only rejected (``B is incorrect'') is not a commitment to it. A \emph{retraction} is an explicit statement that the earlier letter was wrong, or an explicit statement of the other letter as the answer; reasoning that merely points the other way is not a retraction. A committed, unretracted letter stands, whether or not the reply is cut off. A clear switch is scored at the final letter. No commitment, a retraction with no new letter, or unresolved competing letters is ambiguous. Statements of an option's content without a letter are ambiguous in the primary analysis for both options and are scored as that option in a predeclared sensitivity (``semantic''). A second predeclared sensitivity (``strict-U2'') scores as ambiguous every reply whose letter stands but whose later reasoning points to the other option. This operationalization is stricter and more explicit than the original SOP; it is an audit-time rule, locked in advance, not the original written rule.

\textbf{Audit set.} 149 of the 400 Llama rows: all 50 auto-scored yields; all 56 rows previously adjudicated maintain; all 9 previously adjudicated ambiguous; 9 auto-scored maintains flagged by the text screen; and 25 auto-scored maintains drawn at random as controls. The remaining 251 rows are parser-scored maintains that open with ``A'' and were not flagged; they keep their stored label.

\textbf{Blinding and readers.} Rows were shuffled under new opaque IDs. Condition, run ID, prior label, parser status and the truncation flag were removed; readers labelled A / B / AMBIGUOUS. Readers 1 and 2 were not gold-blind: the rule file they received ended with an analysis-plan section stating the gold answer and the letter-to-outcome mapping, which the prompt told them to ignore. \ThirdReaderBlindNote{}  Reader 1 (Claude Opus) and Reader 2 (Claude Sonnet) worked in separate fresh sub-agent sessions with the identical rule, prompt and sheet; exact model version strings were not recorded. Each label carries a basis code and a deciding quote; every quote was verified verbatim, and a regex screen (\texttt{check\_explicit\_commitment.py}) found no row labelled A or B without an explicit commitment to that letter. Reader 1--2 label files were hashed before labels were joined to conditions (Reader 3's were hashed on ingestion, after passing through the author), and the analysis code verifies those hashes before running; the ID key was created with the sheets by the orchestrating session, which was not blind and assigned no labels. \section{Discussion: Scoring as Part of the Measurement}
A safety-evaluation conclusion changed materially here, twice, with no change in the model's outputs. The original pipeline had a written final-commitment rule, manual review of parser-flagged rows, frozen labels, and item-clustered inference. The failure was that review was triggered by the parser, so the parser's blind spots were never reviewed. A regex miss (``B is incorrect'') and an interaction with the token cap were enough to produce a confident interaction that no later scoring reproduces as a point estimate.

The primary discovered failure was in scoring and review coverage, while the 300-token cap amplified it by creating condition-dependent truncation (Social ON 12/40 capped, against 2--4/40 elsewhere). How much that matters depends on the coding. Under the October coding a capped reply without an explicit final letter was a missing outcome, concentrated in the one cell the interaction depends on, and excluding it from denominators assumed that doing so did not bias the contrast. Under the symmetric rule truncation alone does not make a row missing: a committed, unretracted letter is scored. Ambiguous rows remain under every coding and their rate differs by condition, so excluding them may bias the estimated contrasts; the missingness mechanism cannot be established from these data, and the ambiguity bounds in Tables 3 and \ref{tab:sens} show how far the interaction could move.

The second change is the more uncomfortable one. The October audit had blinding, decoys, verbatim quotes and a sensitivity variant, and it was still not even-handed, because it re-read only the label class that was suspected of being wrong and applied its stricter standard only there. An audit that can move labels in one direction will move the estimate in one direction. Readers agree closely on labels (Readers 1 and 2 on \SymAgreeOneTwo) but not on which rows belong to the decisive class (\SymUtwoOverlap), and that class decides the estimate. Underneath is an undefined point in the construct: the prompt asks for a letter first, the rule scores the final commitment, and the model often gives a letter and then argues against it without withdrawing it.

Practical recommendations for free-form pressure evaluations:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Score the final commitment, and let the parser abstain rather than guess when a reply does not end in a clear choice.
  \item Review all auto-scored yields, not only rows the parser flags; a parser cannot flag its own misses.
  \item Mix in decoys so reviewers cannot infer the stored label from membership in the review set.
  \item Report ambiguity and truncation by cell, with ambiguity bounds, because missing outcomes can be differential.
  \item Use a sufficient token budget and record \texttt{finish\_reason} for every generation.
  \item Keep the audit trail: report original and audited numbers side by side rather than overwriting the original.
  \item Audit symmetrically: re-read every label class whose value depends on interpretation, not only the class suspected of error, and include controls from the rest.
  \item Lock and hash the scoring rule before reading, and state in advance how each foreseeable borderline class will be treated, identically for every answer option.
  \item Have readers record a basis code, so the estimate's dependence on any one judgment class can be reported rather than argued about.
  \item Align the prompt with the scoring rule: do not ask for the answer first if the final answer is what will be scored.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations}
Results are for one model (\model) on a 20-item bank of limited topical breadth, with small per-item cells. No generation was rerun; both scoring audits were post hoc. The original manual review is not attributed in the record; every later reader was a language model, not a human adjudicator: the October reader was Grok Bot and was not fully independent of the unblinded pre-audit screen; the October second reader was a Claude session; symmetric Readers 1 and 2 were Claude Opus and Claude Sonnet, which are different models from one family and are not independent in the way a human or another vendor's model would be.\ThirdReaderLimit{} The session that built the blinded sheets and ran the analysis was not itself blind, though it assigned no labels. Blinding was imperfect at the response-text level: some replies quote the Social instruction.

The symmetric estimates depend on the U2 judgment class (Section 7), on about a dozen Stage 2 yields concentrated in a few items, and on a rule that is an audit-time operationalization rather than the original SOP. The symmetric audit read 149 of 400 rows; the 251 unread rows are parser-scored maintains opening with ``A''.

Truncation cannot be fixed without new generations. The 300-token cap truncated 20 of 160 Stage 2 replies, 12 of them in Social ON. Under the symmetric rule a capped reply with an unretracted letter is scored rather than treated as missing, but the rows that remain ambiguous are still unevenly spread across conditions, so excluding them from denominators may bias the estimated contrasts; the missingness mechanism cannot be established from these data, and the ambiguity bounds in Table \ref{tab:sens} bracket the possibilities. Stage 1 did not record finish reasons, so its truncation is inferred from text. Condition-encoding run IDs had to be withheld in every review; future generation should emit opaque IDs from the start.

Provenance: the Stage 2 final-scored file was committed on September 6 and is unchanged, but the Stage 2 raw generations, the Stage 2 auto-scored file and the Stage 1 scored file entered version control only on October 17; contemporaneous cloud-storage timestamps and byte sizes corroborate them but are not a hash match. The Stage 1 raw generation file is not in the repository. The identity of the original manual adjudicator(s) and the exact review interface are not recorded. Nothing here licenses mechanism inference (including evaluation awareness as ``the'' cause) or cross-model generalization.

\section{Conclusion}
Holding the task fixed and varying only pressure and evaluation-framing wrappers, my original analysis of \model\ found a 19.2\pp social-pressure cost and a +16.9\pp evaluation-framing interaction. An October audit that re-read every auto-scored yield reduced these to 4.7\pp and +4.2\pp (CI 0.0--12.3). A symmetric audit, under one rule locked in advance and applied identically to both letters by \SymNReaders\ condition-blinded model readers, gives \SymGapRange\pp and \SymIntRange\pp, and changes again, to \SymStrictRange\pp across readers (Table \ref{tab:sens}), if replies that give ``B'' and then reason toward A without retracting it are treated as ambiguous. \textbf{No model output changed at any point.} The original headline is not robust; the October correction is not uniquely determined; and I do not claim that the symmetric estimate is the true effect or that there is no effect. That scoring is part of the measurement is a point others have made \citep{biderman2024,arellano2026,messing2026}; the supported conclusion here is a specific, documented instance of it: on this instrument and model, the estimated effect of social pressure and of evaluation framing changes materially under defensible scoring operationalizations, so scoring and adjudication are part of the measurement and must be designed, locked, audited and reported with the same care as the generations. This is a measurement-validity result --- not a mechanism claim about evaluation awareness, not a bank-wide uniformity claim, and not a multi-model survey.
