Scoring choices can change what a behavioral evaluation concludes. I document this on a 20-item forced-choice answer-maintenance bank given to Llama 3.3 70B under Neutral and Social pressure wrappers, crossed in Stage 2 with explicit evaluation framing OFF vs ON (160 generations). No generation was rerun: every analysis scores the same frozen replies. (1) The original analysis, a leading-letter parser plus review of parser-flagged rows only, reported a 19.2 pp Neutral-Social gap and a +16.9 pp framing interaction (item-clustered 95% CI 5.1-30.8). (2) Raw replies showed the parser counting a leading "B" as a yield even when the reply rejected B or switched to A. A condition-blinded model re-read of all 50 auto-scored yields gave 4.7 pp and +4.2 pp (CI 0.0-12.3). (3) That audit re-read only stored yields and treated unretracted "A" and "B" differently. Under one rule locked in advance and applied identically to both letters, three condition-blinded model readers labelling 149 rows give gaps of 7.7-10.3 pp and interactions of +9.7 to +12.2 pp. The estimate hinges on one response class: replies that give B, never retract it, then reason toward A. Scoring that class as ambiguous instead of yield moves the interaction to -2.3 to +12.6 pp across readers. Neither the original +16.9 pp nor the +4.2 pp correction is robust, and I claim neither a true effect nor its absence. The same frozen generations support materially different estimates under defensible scoring rules, so scoring and adjudication are part of the measurement, not post-processing.
