# Introduction
*Tightened against frozen claim walls. No new numbers or experiments.*

---

Pressure effects on language-model honesty are hard to interpret when task content, wrapper language, and evaluation cues move together. A drop under a “social” prompt may reflect item difficulty, scoring rules, wrapper wording, or the model’s response to being assessed—none of which are identified if those factors are confounded.

This paper treats that problem as **measurement validity** on a cleaned 20-item honesty bank (gold answers; final-commitment scoring). We ask two staged questions:

1. **Stage 1 (baseline).** With the task held fixed, does changing only the pressure wrapper (Neutral / Social / Training) produce a within-model Neutral−Social honesty gap for Llama 3.3 70B?
2. **Stage 2 (validity test).** On the same frozen Llama instrument, does explicit evaluation framing (OFF vs ON) modify that Neutral−Social gap?

**What we claim.** Stage 1 establishes a within-model social-pressure cost on this instrument (Neutral−Social **19.2 pp**). Stage 2 finds that explicit evaluation framing widens the Neutral−Social gap from **13.0** to **29.9** pp—an interaction of **+16.9 pp** (item-clustered 95% CI **5.1–30.8**)—concentrated under social pressure and in a minority of items, not as a global honesty collapse (Neutral ON **97.5%**).

**What we do not claim.** Evaluation awareness as a mechanism; bank-wide uniform item effects; cross-model or family differences; Training×eval-frame results from Stage 2; or that Stage 2 OFF numerically reproduces Stage 1’s 19.2 pp (OFF is a directional replication only). Discovery-stage nulls on thin eval overlays appear only as design motivation for an isolated OFF/ON contrast.
