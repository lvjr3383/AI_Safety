# Related Work
*Accepted; bibliography locked in `CROSS_PRESSURE_REFERENCES.bib` (cite keys below). Narrow scope only.*

---

**Honesty under pressure.** Behavioral honesty evaluations ask whether models continue to report what they appear to believe when prompts incentivize a preferred false answer. MASK \citep{ren2025mask} is the clearest large-scale example: it elicits beliefs under neutral prompting, then pressures models to contradict those beliefs, explicitly separating honesty from factual accuracy. Earlier truthfulness work such as TruthfulQA \citep{lin2022truthfulqa} primarily measures whether models avoid imitative falsehoods relative to ground truth, which is related but not the same construct as maintaining a stated commitment under wrapper pressure. Much of the surrounding literature varies task content, social cues, and instructional frames together, which makes it hard to separate “the model cannot solve the item” from “the wrapper changed the decision.” Our Stage 1 contribution is narrower: hold a cleaned honesty item bank fixed and change only Neutral / Social / Training wrappers, with final-commitment scoring so recoveries after an early wrong lean count as maintain.

**Evaluation framing and evaluation awareness.** A parallel concern is that models may behave differently when they infer they are being evaluated, audited, or assessed for deployment readiness. Needham et al. \citep{needham2025evalawareness} document substantial evaluation awareness in frontier models—above-chance ability to classify transcripts as evaluation vs deployment—while Laine et al. \citep{laine2024sad} include evaluation awareness as a component of situational awareness and find weaker discrimination in earlier models when cues are thin. Prior discussions often rely on thin cues, correlational contrasts, or multi-factor pilots where eval language is one overlay among several. Stage 2 isolates a single OFF vs ON evaluation-frame contrast on the same frozen instrument used in Stage 1, with OFF free of eval/test/benchmark language and without negation priming (“this is not an evaluation”). We treat framing as a **measurement confound** to be isolated, not as evidence that the model “knows it is being tested.”

**Measurement validity rather than new phenomena.** The present paper is positioned as a measurement-validity study: first establish a within-model pressure contrast on a reusable instrument, then test whether an evaluation-frame factor modifies that contrast. That framing follows construct-validation logic in behavioral measurement—protecting an estimand against a plausible confound—rather than claiming a new capability failure mode or a mechanistic account of why framing changes behavior \citep{cronbach1955construct,messick1995validity}. Recent AI-evaluation work makes the same point explicitly: validity is a property of the claim–evidence pairing, not of a benchmark in isolation \citep{salaudeen2025measurement}. We therefore treat discovery-stage nulls on thin overlays as design motivation, not as a co-equal empirical chapter, and we keep cross-model and mechanism claims outside the claim walls.
